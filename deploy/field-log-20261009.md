# Field commissioning log — 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-09

Raw record of the first two unattended/attended field sessions on the ToF
build, written for citation in the report. Companion data:
`field-log-20261009-observations.csv` (ground-truth observations).

**What this is.** Measurements taken on the deployed hardware, with the method
stated so each can be repeated or disputed. Several supersede figures quoted
earlier in the project, and those supersessions are flagged.

**What this is not.** Not a performance evaluation. The observed-visit sample
is **n = 3 wild visits**, and no rate, recall or precision figure in this
document is supported by that. Where a number looks like a result, read the
caveat attached to it.

---

## 1. The governing fault: the camera was never focused

Camera Module 3 (`imx708`) is an autofocus module. picamera2 leaves it in
`AfMode.Manual` at `LensPosition 1.0` — **one metre** — unless something sets
it. Nothing in `capture/`, `ml/config/` or `deploy/` referenced focus at all
before 2026-10-09. The perch is at **0.114 m**.

So every clip this project has recorded, including all field training footage
captured through this camera, was focused roughly **nine times past the
subject**: sharp background, blurred bird.

### Method

Ran continuous autofocus with a target at the perch and read the lens position
back from frame metadata, 2 s sampling, 60 s run:

| t | LensPosition | implied distance |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2 s | 1.00 | 1.00 m (the stuck default) |
| 12.2 s | 5.56 | 0.180 m |
| 20.2 s | 8.69 | 0.115 m |
| 58.3 s | 8.84 | 0.113 m |

Stable window (t = 20–60 s): 8.15–9.69 dioptres, **median 8.80 → 0.114 m**.
Independently corroborated by the ToF sensor, which ranges the same target at
**10–12 cm**.

Deployed as `camera.lens_position: 8.8` (commit `77f6869`).

### Consequence for the open-set gate

Blur reads as *unfamiliar* to a model trained on sharp frames, so the energy
novelty gate was rejecting real birds. Per-frame clip energies, same camera:

| condition | per-frame energy range | clip novelty | frames clearing −4.9434 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blurred, confirmed Cape White-eye | −2.75 … −5.74 | −4.0903 | 2 of 12 |
| Sharp, human hand | −4.29 … −5.65 | −4.961 | 6 of 12 |
| Sharp, Southern Double-collared | — | **−6.883** | clip cleared by ≈1.9 |

**This supersedes the working hypothesis that the −4.9434 threshold was
miscalibrated.** It was not. It was correctly reporting that blurred frames
look unfamiliar. The threshold was briefly loosened to −3.5 on 2026-10-09 to
get a test through, then restored once focus was fixed; both changes were Pi-
local and uncommitted.

## 2. The trigger asked the wrong question

**This supersedes section 2 as first written (ROI geometry) and part of section
4.** Both were symptoms.

`motion.detection_range_mm` asks "is anything within 500 mm?". **The perch is
bolted in the beam at ~13 cm**, so the answer is permanently yes. The gate
latched on the first poll after service start and never produced another rising
edge; a bird landing on an already-triggered sensor generated no event at all.

Three things follow, and they explain the whole afternoon:

1. Every absolute range behaved identically -- 300 mm gave 44 rising edges,
   1300 mm gave 45. That is the signature of this fault, not of a badly chosen
   threshold, and it was in the data from 2026-10-06 unread.
2. Narrowing `roi_size` appeared to fix the empty-port triggers. It worked by
   aiming the beam away from the place birds land, which is why it cost two
   observed visits.
3. `max_hold_seconds`, added to *log* a stuck gate, became the only mechanism
   admitting detections at all -- on a 60 s timer unrelated to any bird. The
   two true positives in section 6 arrived through it by accident.

The warning text written for that valve already named the cause ("check whether
something is parked in the beam, e.g. the perch itself") and it was read as a
hypothetical for several hours.

### The fix: baseline subtraction

`motion.baseline_margin_mm` (default 20 mm) switches the source to the model the
camera's `IdleBackgroundGate` already uses: learn the resting distance of the
empty scene as a running median of clear-state readings, fire when a reading
departs from it by at least the margin. Readings taken while the gate is
detected never enter the median, so a bird that sits at the port cannot become
part of the baseline. The absolute model is kept for a mounting where the beam
really is empty at rest, and for the replay tests.

The margin is **symmetric** (nearer *or* farther). A perching bird reads nearer
than the bare rod; whichever static object dominates the return going away reads
farther. Both mean the scene changed, and picking a direction needs geometry
this mounting has not had measured.

**Follow-up, 2026-10-09:** the Pi was reachable again and the baseline trigger
was deployed with a **7 mm margin**. With the full 16 × 16 ROI, a 30 s empty
trace returned 282/283 valid readings, median 11.9 cm and range 11.4–12.5 cm.
In a timed 30 s test, an object held at the bird landing spot shifted the
steady return from about 11.9 to 13.0 cm for 14 s, then it returned to 12.0 cm
on removal. The original 20 mm margin would miss this ~11 mm shift; 7 mm clears
the observed empty-scene spread and detects this object. The live service
learned a 12.1 cm resting baseline after deployment. **No bird-trigger recall
is established by a hand/object test.** Recheck the margin after mount changes
and against observed wild visits.

An object placed at the landing spot with the live capture service running
produced one ToF admission at 17:57:51. The 8.5 s clip completed; the classifier
mistook the hand/object for a sunbird and published visit #20. That test row and
its two media files were deleted after the operator approved cleanup. No
additional static-perch trigger appeared in the first minute after restart.

Note what this does *not* fix: nothing above recovers the visits already missed,
and trigger recall remains unquantified (section 6).

### Margin fitted, 2026-10-10, and the instrument that was wrong first

Both earlier margin fits (7 mm on 2026-10-09, and 20 mm recommended on the
morning of 2026-10-10) were **artifacts of a broken measuring script**.
`deploy/tof_baseline.py` hand-rolled the trigger state machine in its replay
loop instead of calling `poll_once`, and omitted `max_hold_seconds`. The
replayed gate therefore latched on its first detection and never re-armed,
reporting exactly **1 event for every margin from 5 to 15 mm** -- which reads
as "quiet" and means "stuck". The tell was there to be seen: the script claimed
1.1 false triggers/min while the live service was producing 6.7/min on an empty
feeder, a 6x disagreement.

The replay now drives the real `poll_once`. Refitted against a 180 s
empty-scene trace at the feeder in SE wind:

| margin | false triggers/min (replay) | live |
|---|---|---|
| 5 mm | 15.7 | — |
| 7 mm | 5.2 | 6.7 measured |
| 8 mm | 2.1 | — |
| **10 mm** | **0.3** | **0.67 measured** |
| 15 / 20 / 30 mm | 0.3 | — |

Resting distance median **118 mm, stdev 3.0 mm**, p1 113, p99 129. The 3.0 mm
supersedes a 6.6 mm figure from a 60 s sample earlier the same morning; that
window was too short. Nothing above 10 mm improves on it, because a single
near-side outlier at 73 mm fires at any margin. Deployed at **10 mm**: live
rising edges fell from 20 to 2 per three minutes.

### Birds read FARTHER than the baseline, not nearer

The departure log added with the 10 mm margin settled a question that had been
guessed at in code comments. Both triggers in the first three minutes were real
birds, and both departed in the **positive** direction:

| | departure | result |
|---|---|---|
| Cape White-eye | **+13 mm** | published, species, conf 0.880, novelty −6.69 |
| Cape Bulbul | **+11 mm** | correctly discarded, not a capture target |
| human hand, 2026-10-09 | +11 mm | — |

The resting return is the near furniture; a body at the port scatters the beam
so the dominant return comes from behind it. **A nearer-only margin, which the
code comments had proposed as an optimisation, would have missed both birds.**

This also fixes the upper bound in place: a bird clears the 10 mm margin by only
**1–3 mm**, and the empty-scene wind tail reaches +11 mm. Wind and birds sit in
the same band on the far side. The margin cannot be raised without losing
visits, so this is a standing limit of the ToF trigger rather than something
further tuning removes. **No recall figure follows from two system-reported
birds** -- neither was observer-confirmed, so neither appears in the
ground-truth CSV.

### Superseded: trigger geometry calibrated against a proxy

The VL53L1X region of interest was narrowed to exclude the feeder bottle, which
sits **nearer the sensor (9–13 cm) than the perch (~20 cm)**. Because the sensor
returns one dominant distance per reading, a nearer permanent object masks the
perch entirely and nothing downstream can recover it.

Narrowing worked for the bottle and **cost real birds**:

| `roi_size` | span at 0.114 m | empty-port triggers | observed wild visits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 4 | ~1.4 cm | 0 in 3 min | **1 missed** |
| 8 × 8 | ~2.8 cm | 0 in 11 min | 1 caught, **1 missed** |
| 16 × 16 (full ~27°) | — | 3 admitted + 1 cooldown-dropped in 2 min | — |

Both narrow settings were validated against a **human fist**, which fills a
1.4 cm window where a sunbird does not. That proxy could not have revealed the
difference, and the failure was silent: a missed visit produces no log line of
any kind.

### Why the full array is nonetheless the right default

Every one of the 16 × 16 empty-port triggers was scored `unknown` by the novelty
gate and **retained, never published**. Not one became a false visit record. So
the two errors are not comparable in cost:

- **false trigger** → a discarded clip, some card wear, and a retained frame
  that is useful refit data
- **missed visit** → unrecoverable, and invisible in the logs

Narrowing moves the precision burden onto the sensor, which is the wrong layer.
The trigger should be generous; the classifier and allowlist are the precision
filter. Settled at `roi_size: 16` (commit `780d2b5`).

**Caveat on the trigger-rate column:** the mount shifted at least three times
during 2026-10-09 (twice observed, once inferred from the bottle leaving the
beam during the PSU swap). Each shift invalidated the previous ROI measurement.
Those rates are therefore not comparable to each other as a controlled series.

## 4. Trigger-source faults found and fixed

Two defects in `ToFMotionSource`, both measured on the deployed hardware:

**Dropout re-triggering.** Roughly 50% of sensor reads return invalid at the
feeder, and the VL53L1X reports "nothing in range" *as* an invalid read — so a
dropout mid-visit is indistinguishable from the bird leaving. Releasing on the
first one turned a single continuous presence into **44 triggers in 60 s**.
Changing `detection_range_mm` does not help: 300 mm gave 44 rising edges,
1300 mm gave 45. Fixed by damping only the release (`release_seconds: 1.0`);
replayed against the measured trace, 60 s of continuous presence yields 147
events at 0 s, 8 at 0.5 s, **1 at 1.0 s** (commit `a1d8604`).

**Silent blindness.** A target parked in the beam holds the trigger down
permanently and the service stops recording with nothing in the log to
distinguish that from a quiet afternoon. It was misdiagnosed twice from logs
alone on 2026-10-06. `max_hold_seconds: 60` now logs a warning naming the
distance and re-arms; simulated against a perch parked in the beam for 10 min,
**1 event and silence before, 10 events and 10 warnings after** (commit
`c11fec6`).

## 5. Thermal and resource telemetry

Continuous logging added 2026-10-09 (`deploy/telemetry.py`,
`birdcam-telemetry.service`, 30 s interval → `var/telemetry/health.csv`).
Before this there was no health record at all; earlier figures were hand
spot-checks.

Baseline and one record-classify-publish cycle on a real sunbird:

| | idle | during capture | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| SoC temperature | 48.7 °C | 51.6 °C | +2.9 °C |
| capture RSS | 108.2 MiB | 146.0 MiB | +37.8 MiB |
| CPU (30 s mean) | 0.5% | 6.5% | +6.0 pp |
| `get_throttled` | `0x0` | `0x0` | — |

ARM clock 1.80 GHz, core 0.9060 V throughout. New 5.1 V supply fitted
2026-10-09; no undervoltage flag, including the latched bits 16–19.

### Power consumption is not measurable here

**A Pi 4B has no onboard power sensor.** Watts are not obtainable in software at
any sample rate. `core_volt` is the SoC core rail, not input power, and the
throttle bits only indicate whether the 5 V input sagged past the undervoltage
threshold. **Any power figure in the report must come from an inline USB-C power
meter**, not from this CSV. The warning is repeated in the module docstring and
the unit file.

### Clock caveat

The Pi has no RTC. It boots at the last-saved time and logs in that frame until
NTP corrects it, which can move timestamps by days — the 2026-10-06 service
startup lines are stamped in restored-clock time. The CSV carries `synced` (drop
rows where 0) and `uptime_s` (monotonic); **prefer `uptime_s` as the x-axis**.

The first session where the trigger, the focus, the classifier, the gate and the
allowlist all worked at once across multiple visits. Configuration: ToF

`lens_position 8.8`, energy threshold −4.9434.

| visit | time | species | conf | novelty | ToF departure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23 | 10:27:21 | Cape White-eye | 0.880 | −6.69 | — |
| 24 | 10:30:27 | Southern Double-collared Sunbird | 0.829 | −6.10 | — |
| 25 | 10:42:36 | Amethyst Sunbird | 0.807 | −6.39 | +11 mm |
| 26 | 10:43:35 | Amethyst Sunbird | 0.714 | −6.76 | +13 mm |
| 27 | 10:45:56 | Cape White-eye | 0.920 | −7.49 | +43 mm |
| 28 | 10:50:31 | Cape White-eye | 0.891 | −7.02 | +13 mm |
| 29 | 10:52:45 | Cape White-eye | 0.897 | −7.56 | +12 mm |
| 30 | 11:00:20 | Cape White-eye | 0.911 | −7.28 | +26 mm |
| 31 | 11:03:05 | Southern Double-collared Sunbird | 0.856 | −6.56 | +17 mm |

**All nine are operator-confirmed correct.** Visits 25 and 26 are 59 s apart and
may be one individual returning, so this is nine correct *publishes* across at
least eight visits, not necessarily nine independent visits.

### The operator reported no missed sightings

Across 10:27:21–11:03:05 the operator watched the feeder and reported that every
bird seen produced a publish. **Zero observed false negatives.**

This is the first positive evidence on trigger recall, which every previous
session left unquantified and §6 names as the largest open question in the
deployment. It is **not a recall rate**: the operator's attention window was not
logged, so the observation is not duty-cycle controlled, and it covers one
morning, one site and three species.

### The novelty gate separates cleanly at this focus

| | confidence | novelty |
|---|---|---|
| 9 publishes | 0.714 – 0.920 | **−6.10 … −7.56** |
| 6 retained as `unknown` | — | **−4.36 … −5.03** |

The −4.9434 threshold falls in the gap between two non-overlapping clusters,
with ~1.1 units of margin to the nearest publish. Compare §1, where blurred
frames from a confirmed Cape White-eye produced −4.09 and were rejected: the
separation is a consequence of fixing the focus, not of retuning the threshold.
`is_unknown` is decided by a per-frame vote, so a clip can carry a mean novelty
past the threshold and still be voted unknown (10:33:19, mean −5.03).

### Amethyst at species level

`ml/RUNNING.md` records female Amethyst as the model's weakest real case — under
the clean-20260921 calibration its species labels got *worse*, 1 of 5
species-correct, with one female published as the wrong species. Two
operator-confirmed species-level Amethyst publishes at 0.807 and 0.714, against
a per-class threshold of 0.43, are the first evidence against that. Two clips
are not a rate and the retrain option in `RUNNING.md` still stands.

### Trigger cost at 10 mm

Six retained `unknown` clips in the same 25 minutes, review buffer 270/600, so
the fitted 0.3 false triggers/min holds in the field. One of those retains was a
**+11 mm** departure at 10:48:13 — the same magnitude as confirmed visit 25.
That is direct evidence that +11 mm is the boundary itself: it admits real birds
and admits whatever that was. Confirmed bird departures span **+10 to +43 mm**, all positive (A34), with
+10 mm firing only because the comparison is `>=`. A bird departing +9 mm
would be invisible and would produce no log line of any kind.

The first negative departure on a stable baseline appeared at 10:54:52, −10 mm
(109 mm against 119 mm). It scored `unknown` at novelty −4.5503 and was retained.
One case only, but consistent with A34: the far side carries the birds, and the
near side has so far carried nothing.

## 8. What the report should not claim from this session

1. **No recall or precision rate.** The 2026-10-09 figures rest on n = 3 wild
   visits across three configurations on a moving mount. The 2026-10-10 morning
   run (§7a) is much stronger — nine publishes, nine operator-confirmed correct,
   no observed misses, one fixed configuration — but still supports no rate: the
   observer's attention window was not logged, so it is not duty-cycle
   controlled, and it is one morning, one site, three species. Quote it as
   "nine of nine correct with no observed misses over 36 minutes", never as a
   precision or recall figure.
2. **No power figure.** Not measurable on this hardware.
3. **No comparison between the ROI trigger rates** as a controlled series.
4. **Cape White-eye confidences are uncalibrated.**
5. **Every calibration predating 2026-10-09 was fitted on blurred frames** —
   the per-class thresholds, the energy threshold and the INT8 quantisation
   calibration all used footage from this camera at the wrong focus, or phone
   video of a different scene. `birdcam.eval.calibrate_capture` should be rerun
   against labelled clips from the corrected camera before any of those numbers
   is quoted as a deployment result.

Item 5 is the substantive piece of work left, and it is now possible:
`publish.retain_unknown: true` keeps the gate's own rejects, and
`storage.max_review_clips: 600` stops them being evicted, so the labelled set
accumulates with normal running.
